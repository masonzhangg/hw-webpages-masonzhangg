Summary

This project implements a realistic glass shattering simulation using computer graphics techniques. The simulation models the physical properties of glass, including fracture propagation and fragment dynamics, to produce break patterns. The goal is to render shattering effects that accurately reflect how glass behaves under impact.

Problem Description

Many video games require glass breaking, whether that'd be for players jumping out windows, breaking windows to get a better shot, or breaking and replacing windows for a better looking house. However, it is surprisingly difficult to simulate convincingly in real-time, as poorly executed shattering effects are noticeable and break immersion for the player. Glass shattering is a complex phenomenon that demands accurate modeling of both fracture geometry (how cracks propagate across a surface based on impact force and point of contact) and the resulting fragment dynamics, including how shards fly and interact with the environment. To address this, we will implement a realistic glass shattering simulation that combines procedural fracture generation with physics-based fragment simulation. Fracture patterns will be generated using Voronoi decomposition to mimic the natural crack networks that form in real glass, based on where and how hard the impact occurs. The resulting shards will behave like physical objects, responding to gravity, collisions, and rotation. Finally, the glass fragments will be rendered with transparency, refraction, and reflections to make them look as realistic as possible.

What We Plan To Deliver

Our baseline goal is to deliver a demo in which a user can trigger a glass shattering event and observe the result in a realistic and visually convincing manner. This includes properly generated fracture patterns, physically simulated shards that respond to gravity and collisions, and glass-like rendering with transparency and reflections. To measure the performance of our system, we will track and display the frame rate during the shattering simulation, giving us a metric to evaluate how well our implementation holds up under the computational load of real-time fragment physics and rendering.

What We Hope To Deliver

If development goes smoothly, we hope to add onto the demo, transforming it into a fully real-time, resettable experience where the glass automatically resets after shattering, allowing the user to break it repeatedly. This would demonstrate not only the visual quality of our simulation but also its stability and consistency across multiple shattering events. This would bring our project much closer to something resembling a real game engine feature, where glass breaking needs to be triggered reliably and repeatedly without degrading in performance or quality over time.

Schedule

Week 1 (03/29 - 04/04): Research, Writing Proposal, and Planning. We will be looking into Voronoi-based algorithms and find relevant literature and implementations that we can reference. We will also be looking into rigid body physics simulation techniques and glass rendering methods.

Week 2 (04/05 - 04/11): Begin Implementation. We will begin implementing the core Voronoi decomposition system to generate fracture patterns on a glass mesh. We will also work on splitting the glass geometry into individual shard meshes based on the Voronoi regions. The physics simulation and the rendering pipeline will also be set up, with gravity, rigid body motion, and basic collision detection. Basic specular shading and transparency will be implemented too.

Week 3 (04/12 - 04/18): Implement Enough for Milestone Video/Slides/Webpage. We will integrate the fracture generation, physics, and rendering systems together so that a complete shattering event can be triggered and displayed. We will debug if necessary and refine the shard physics so fagments can behave correctly after the break. We will add refraction and reflections to the glass as well. We will also need to make sure the fracturing behaves correctly and are clean.

Week 4 (04/19 - 04/25): Implementation and Testing. We will be performance testing, measuring the frames during the shattering and rendering, maybe identifying bottlenecks. We will be optimizing the algorithms. If time allows, we will work on the reset mechanic that allows the glass to be continuously reshattered.

Week 5 (04/26 - 05/02): Final Slides/Video/Presentation Preparation + Submission. We will wrap up the project with making the slides and prepare for the in-person presentation on May 5. Everyone will contribute to making the webpage, the video, and the slides. We will also finish with the peer review forms also due May 5.
