Summary of Project

This project implements a realistic glass shattering simulation using computer graphics techniques. The simulation models the physical properties of glass, including fracture propagation and fragment dynamics, to produce break patterns. The goal is to render shattering effects that accurately reflect how glass behaves under impact.

Summary of Accomplishments

We built a real-time interactive glass shattering simulation using OpenGL, the CGL framework. The fracture system generates Voronoi-based break patterns by distributing seed points across a glass panel (roughly 20% clustered near the impact site via a Gaussian distribution and 80% uniformly scattered). Then it constructs a 2D Voronoi diagram through iterative half-plane clipping to produce convex polygonal cells that represent individual shard outlines. Each 2D cell is extruded into a closed 3D mesh with front and back faces plus side walls, using the panel's thickness and world-space coordinate frame, giving every shard proper geometry for both rendering and physics. A rigid body physics engine assigns each shard a mass and inertia tensor derived from its mesh geometry, then integrates linear and angular motion under gravity with damping. Ground-plane collision detection checks every vertex of every shard against the floor, and a two-stage response first bounces shards with horizontal scatter on initial contact, then snaps them flat and puts them to sleep on second contact to prevent bouncing and jittering. The rendering pipeline uses a custom GLSL glass shader that computes Schlick Fresnel reflectance, dual-light specular highlights, and environment-mapped reflections sampled from a loaded background texture, all composited at very low base alpha to achieve a transparent glass look. A separate solid shader handles the ground plane, and a fullscreen quad shader draws the background. The interactive demo allows for clicking on the glass panel to trigger the initial shatter (spawning up to 250 shards), and then clicking on individual fallen shards to re-fracture them by generating a new Voronoi diagram clipped to the parent cell's polygon while inheriting the parent's velocity and spin. The demo also supports pausing and resetting using "P" and "R" respectively, and a real-time FPS count.

Preliminary Results

When the program runs, it launches a window, renders a glass panel with a sky background, and a ground plane. There is also interactive shattering. On the initial shatter, there are 250 shards, but then it increases to 500 shards on the next shatters.

Reflection on Progress

As of April 16, our progress lines up well with what we have produced at the start of the project. We completed the proposal pretty quickly and started moving onto implementation. The Voronoi-based fracture generation, extrusion of shards into 3D meshes, rigid-body physics with gravity and ground interaction, and a glass-oriented shading pass with Fresnel, specular highlights, and environment-mapped reflections are all wired together in an interactive demo where users can shatter the panel, watch fragments fall, and subdivide shards further. We are ahead of pace for the Week 3 Milestone and there is definitely enough time left to refine the visuals and the realistic factor of the glass. We can spend week 4 on optimizing our algorithms and making our program faster, before the final video, slides, and presentation. The remaining work is pretty much testing and optimizing as we have the core systems already.
